Assumptions

Why these rates: timing-signal emails to a named buyer typically reply above generic cold email (1–3%), so 8% reply with half interested is a target, not a given. The pilot's job is to measure the real rates in 30 days.

That is about {{ f.emails_per_weekday|round|int }} emails a weekday, so {{ f.inboxes_needed }} warmed inboxes at ≤30 a day each.

Tools

  • This tool: weekly research run on ~{{ (f.week.new_prospects * 1.5)|round|int }} companies (about 2 in 3 come back with a sourced signal).
  • Instantly: sending, inbox warm-up and rotation, reply tracking, unsubscribe handling.
  • HubSpot CRM (free): pipeline Researched → Emailed → Replied → Call → Proposal → Won.
  • Calendly: the 15-minute call link, set to Sydney time.

Staying out of spam

  • Send from a separate domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set, warm it up for 2–3 weeks, ≤30 sends per inbox per day.
  • Plain text: no images or attachments, at most one link, open tracking off on the first email.
  • Clean list: role-relevant published addresses only, verified before sending, bounces under 2%, unsubscribes honoured right away.

Every Monday, report 3 numbers

  1. New prospects emailed (target {{ f.week.new_prospects|round|int }}/week)
  2. Interested-reply rate (target {{ (a.reply_rate * a.positive_share)|pct }} of prospects)
  3. Discovery calls booked (target {{ '%.1f'|format(f.week.calls) }}/week)

Top 2 risks

1. Not enough signal-qualified companies. {{ f.week.new_prospects|round|int }} new prospects a week is more than two segments in two cities produce with fresh signals. Response: add a third segment (universities or not-for-profits), add ASX announcements and event-venue calendars (ICC Sydney, MCEC) as sources, and reuse "no signal" companies when they announce something.
2. Replies come in below 4% (deliverability, or a Bengaluru-founded studio seen as offshore). Response: check domain health and inbox placement first, A/B test subject lines, lead with Sydney presence and 2 local case studies, and add a LinkedIn touch before the follow-up.
